How it plays

Build as high as you can. That is the whole game; the sway is what makes it one.

Tap to drop

A bar slides back and forth over the top of the tower. Tap and it lands. Whatever hangs over the edge is sliced off and tumbles away, and the next bar is that much narrower. The slider starts slow and speeds up as you climb.

The tower sways

The taller the tower, the more its top moves, and the bar you drop doesn't move with it. A sloppy drop kicks the tower toward the overhang; clean drops let it settle. A good drop times the slide and the sway together.

Slide or Drop

Slide lands the bar where you tap. Drop runs the bar one bar higher and lets it fall onto wherever the top has swayed to by the time it gets there. Same slices, same lives, same slow-to-fast slider; each mode keeps its own best.

PERFECT

Land the seam exactly and the tower calms, the bar flashes gold, and the sliver you lost last time comes back. Chain them and the chime climbs with the streak: PERFECT ×2, ×3, ×4. On iPhone, every perfect taps the Taptic Engine.

Three lives, five ways to go out, thirty medals

A miss costs a life. Lose the third and the top of the tower tips over and blows up, one of five ways: a fireball, fireworks, a shockwave, confetti or popcorn. Thirty medals carry across both modes: tall towers at 5, 10, 15 … 100 bars (round coins) and perfect streaks at 3, 6, 9 … 30 in a row (star-edged seals), bronze to gold. Earned mid-run they only chime; they appear on the game-over card.

A world for every season

Thirteen hand-made worlds, each with its own song. The holiday worlds arrive on their dates; any world can be picked any time from Skins. Every one opens with something happening in its first ten seconds, and its story climbs with the tower.

Original music on real instruments

One theme, a waltz in six-eight, arranged thirteen ways. The cartoon worlds' songs are rendered from CC0 recordings of real instruments (saxophone, French horn, euphonium, piano and drums wherever they fit a world); the two neon worlds are built on our own synths (Cyberpunk puts a real tenor sax over them). Every song keeps pace with the game: past 40 bars it speeds up with the slider, time-stretched so the pitch never changes.

Support

Sway Tower is made by one person. For a bug, a question or something you'd like to see, write to swaytower.support@gmail.com and say which device and which world.

How do I play?

Tap PLAY, then tap anywhere to drop the bar (or press Space on a keyboard). It slides back and forth over the tower; whatever hangs over the edge is sliced off, so the next bar is narrower. Land it exactly for a PERFECT: the tower calms and the sliver comes back. A drop that misses the tower costs one of three lives. The taller the tower, the more it sways, so time the slide and the sway together.

What is the difference between Slide and Drop?

Pick the mode on the title screen. In Slide the bar runs across the top of the tower and lands where you tap. In Drop it runs one bar higher and falls when you tap, landing on wherever the swaying top has moved to by then. The rules, slices, lives and speed are the same; each mode keeps its own best.

How do I turn off the music, the sound effects or the haptics?

Open Settings: the Skins button on the title screen, or the Settings button at the top left during a run. Under Sound, switch Music, Effects and Haptics on or off (Haptics shows on devices with a Taptic Engine). Under Motion, Reduce motion stops the screen shake and flashes; Auto follows your device's Reduce Motion setting. The Sound button on the title screen turns the music and the effects off or on together.

How do I pick a world?

Skins on the title screen lists all thirteen. Auto, the default, switches to a holiday world on its dates and shows Sky Islands (listed as Classic) the rest of the year; pick any world to keep it.

Where are my bests and medals saved?

On your device only, in the app's own storage. There is no account and nothing is sent anywhere, so they don't move between devices; your device's own backup (iCloud or a computer) includes them. Deleting the app deletes them.

Does it need the internet?

No. Everything, the music included, ships in the app, so it plays in Airplane Mode.

Privacy Policy

Sway Tower, the app for iPhone and iPad, and this website. Effective October 28, 2026.

In short: Sway Tower collects no data. The developer, Turk White, does not collect, receive, store, sell or share any personal information from the app.

What the app collects

Nothing. The app has no account or sign-in of its own, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no crash reporting and no server. It makes no network connections of its own and works offline. It contains no third-party code that collects data (it is built with Capacitor, an open-source app framework that runs inside the app and sends nothing anywhere), and it shares nothing with anyone.

The only ways out of the app are links you tap yourself: Support and Privacy in Settings open this website in Safari, and Rate Sway Tower shows Apple's rating prompt or opens the App Store.

What stays on your device

To work, the app saves a few things in its own storage on your device: your best score in each mode, the medals you've earned (with when and in which mode), the mode and world you picked, your settings (music, sound effects, haptics, motion), and a few reminders of its own, such as whether it has already asked you for a rating. None of it leaves your device or reaches the developer. It is included in your device's backups as your iOS settings decide, and deleting the app deletes it.

Sharing a picture of your tower

The Share button on the game-over card draws a picture of your tower and score on your device and hands it to the iOS share sheet, which sends it only where you choose. If you choose Save Image, iOS asks once for permission to add to your photo library. That permission is add-only: the app can save the picture but cannot see, read or change your photos. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Photos › Sway Tower.

Ratings

Now and then the app may show Apple's standard prompt to rate it. Apple runs that prompt; the developer sees only the public ratings and reviews on the App Store.

Children

Sway Tower is rated 12+ (13+ on iOS 26 and later) and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13.
