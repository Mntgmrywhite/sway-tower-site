How It Plays

Build as high as you can. The goal is simple. The sway is what makes it challenging.

Tap to place

A bar glides back and forth over your tower. Tap, and it lands. Anything hanging over the edge gets sliced off and tumbles away, so every slip leaves you a little less room. The bar starts slow and picks up speed as you climb.

The tower sways

Down low, your tower barely moves. Build higher and its top starts to swing, while the bar gliding over it doesn't swing along. An off-center placement sends the tower swaying even farther. Clean ones let it settle. The trick is timing the slide and the sway together.

Slide or Drop

In Slide, the bar lands right where you tap. In Drop, it glides one bar higher and falls when you tap, so aim for where the swaying top will be when it lands, not where it is now. Same slices, same lives, same slow-to-fast slider, and each mode keeps its own best.

PERFECT

Line a bar up exactly and the seam flashes, the bar glows gold, the tower calms down, and a sliver you lost earlier comes back. String them together and the chime climbs with your streak: PERFECT ×2, ×3, ×4. On iPhone, you'll feel every one.

3 lives, 5 grand finales, 30 medals

Miss the tower completely and you lose a life. Lose all three and the top of your tower tips over and goes out with a bang, one of five ways: a fireball, fireworks, a shockwave, a burst of confetti or a round of popcorn. Along the way, earn 30 medals in bronze, silver and gold, in either mode: round coins for towers of 5, 10, 15 … 100 bars, and star-edged seals for 3, 6, 9 … 30 perfects in a row. Medals won mid-run just chime softly and wait for you on the game-over card.

A World for Every Season

Explore 13 handcrafted worlds, each with its own music, atmosphere, and little surprises to discover.

From floating islands and neon cities to haunted houses and holiday celebrations, every world has something new to see as your tower climbs higher.

Seasonal worlds appear automatically around their holidays, but you can explore any world whenever you like.

Sky Islands

The default

Your climb begins on a little floating island, roots and vines dangling beneath it, a spring spilling off its edge and a kite tugging at its string. Far below, tractors plod across a storybook countryside.

Build up through the clouds and drift past islands of cottages, windmills and waterfalls. Watch the sun sink into a sea of clouds, then keep going into a starry night of sky lanterns, shimmering aurora and shooting stars.

Toy Kingdom

Any time

Step into a kingdom built from toy blocks, where a wooden rainbow arches over the town, toy soldiers drum outside the fort and a freshly wound mouse waddles off toward the gate.

Ten seconds in, the toy rocket blasts off its pad, corkscrews into the clouds and comes back down to land, ready to go again. Climb above the block clouds to a sleepy moon, a ringed toy planet and constellations shaped like toys.

Synthwave

Any time

Cruise into an 80s sunset, where a striped sun glows over a neon grid, two tall palms frame the view and an arcade lights up the roadside.

Every so often the driver strolls out of the arcade, hops into the chrome car, lays down a smoky burnout, spins donuts out on the grid and races off into the city. Climb past the sunset into a violet night of neon clouds, flamingos and a ringed planet.

Cyberpunk

Any time

Welcome to a rain-soaked megacity after dark, where neon signs shimmer on wet streets, a hover taxi idles overhead and searchlights sweep through the smog.

Keep an eye on the street racer: moments after you start, it peels out through a puddle and soaks the umbrella walker, then comes back around to do it all again. Climb past glowing towers, drones and sky gardens, up through the smog and into a clear, starry night.

Halloween

Oct 1 – Nov 1

Welcome to Main Street on Halloween night, where crooked buildings, restless graves, and strange shadows set the scene.

Your tower climbs in candy colors (pumpkins, bats, candy corn and crows) past glowing windows into the night. Watch the Headless Horseman race across the moon as you climb through storm clouds and into a surreal sky of upside-down islands.

Thanksgiving

Nov 2 – 30

Settle into a cozy hollow on a late-November afternoon, where a big orange tree lets go of its leaves, turkeys peck about the yard and a white church steeple peeks out from the little town beyond.

Out on the lawn, the townsfolk carry out the plates, the turkey and a pie, sit down to supper, clear it away and set the table all over again. Climb as the sun goes down, a pale moon rises and the geese head south.

Christmas

Dec 1 – 27

Step into Dickens's snowy London at dusk, drawn like a 1950s Christmas card, where candles glow in the windows, gas lamps warm the snow and carolers sing by lantern light.

Find Scrooge & Marley's sooty counting house, spot Marley's face in the door knocker and watch the carriage go round and round your tower. Then climb through the snowy night into a golden Christmas morning.

New Year's Eve

Dec 28 – Jan 6

Ring in the New Year at a 1920s Manhattan party, where gold searchlights sweep over Art Deco towers and a mansion party glows across the bay, a green light at the end of its dock.

Count down the final minute as the ball drops, then watch the skyline erupt in fireworks at midnight. A champagne tower sparkles on the terrace, giant billboard eyes blink across the water, and the party goes on all night as you climb.

Mardi Gras

The six days to Fat Tuesday

Grab a spot on Bourbon Street, where lacy iron balconies drip with beads and bunting, a family tosses necklaces down to the kids below and the corner café serves up beignets.

Within seconds the parade rolls by: a brass band second-lining, papier-mâché floats with masked riders and a jester on stilts. Climb into the night for fireworks over the river as the Carnival Queen and the grinning King Gator rise out of the clouds.

Valentine's Day

Feb 7 – 15

Fall for Paris at the golden hour, where your tower rises from a heart-shaped fountain in a rose garden, beside a chocolatier, a florist and a little café.

Watch a marble cherub spring to life and pop a heart balloon with an arrow, two swans glide together into a heart, and a couple swap a card at the café. Climb through the evening mist to find Paris standing above the clouds, with fireworks of hearts after dark.

St. Patrick's Day

Mar 10 – 18

Wander into the west of Ireland in springtime, drawn like an illuminated manuscript, where a rainbow arches over an old round tower and lambs race through the pasture.

A leprechaun hauls his pot of gold to the rainbow's end and keeps a careful eye on it, while an old box player strikes up a jig and raises a toast to the gold. Then climb through a spring shower to a second little rainbow and a starry night.

Independence Day

Jun 28 – Jul 7

Celebrate the Fourth on a small-town Main Street straight off a vintage postcard, with flags over the storefronts, bunting on the bandstand and lemonade on the picnic lawn.

The parade marches in right away: a drum major and band, a fire-truck float full of waving kids and Uncle Sam on stilts. Climb into a summer sky of towering clouds and stunt planes, then stay for the fireworks blooming over the rooftops after dark.

Oktoberfest

Sep 12 – 30

Join the festival on a meadow under the Alps, where your tower stands on the bandstand of a blue-and-white striped tent, the oompah band plays beside it and the stalls are piled with pretzels and gingerbread hearts.

Draft horses pull a garlanded barrel wagon past, a waitress carries a tray of foaming steins and a pair of dancers slap out a schuhplattler. Climb as the valley mist rolls in, the Ferris wheel lights up at dusk and a pretzel balloon drifts by on the way to a starry Alpine night.

A Song for Every World

One original melody, reimagined for 13 different worlds. Sky Islands plays it as a sweeping waltz, Toy Kingdom winds it up into a toy-box march, Mardi Gras hands it to a New Orleans brass band, New Year's Eve dances it to a Roaring Twenties foxtrot and St. Patrick's Day kicks it into a set of Irish jigs. Every world has a song of its own, and the music keeps pace with you: build past 40 bars and it speeds up right along with the slider.

Behind the Music

Every song grows from the same theme, a waltz in six-eight, arranged from scratch for its world. The cartoon worlds are played on real instruments, rendered note by note from CC0 recordings, which are free for anyone to use, and every recording is tuned so the melody sits just right.

Saxophone, French horn, euphonium, piano and drums are Turk White's favorite instruments, so you'll hear them wherever they fit a world. There is no CC0 recording of a euphonium to be had, so the one you hear is a trombone and a tuba playing the same notes, mellowed into one warm voice.

The two neon worlds run on a synthesizer and drum machine built for the game, and Cyberpunk lays a real tenor sax over them. The same synth supplies the few sounds no recording could, like Halloween's werewolf howl and Toy Kingdom's music box.

When the music picks up speed past 40 bars, it climbs step by step, up to about a third faster. Each song is time-stretched as it goes, so the pitch never changes, and each step waits for the next bar line, so the beat never stumbles.

Support

Sway Tower is made by one person, Turk White, who would love to hear from you. Found a bug, have a question, or want to suggest something? Write to swaytower.support@gmail.com and mention which device you're on and which world you were playing.

How do I play?

Tap PLAY, then tap anywhere to place the bar (or press Space on a keyboard). The bar glides back and forth over your tower, and anything hanging over the edge gets sliced away, so the next bar is narrower. Line it up exactly for a PERFECT: the tower calms down and a lost sliver comes back. Miss the tower completely and you lose one of your 3 lives. The higher you build, the more the tower sways, so time the slide and the sway together.

What's the difference between Slide and Drop?

Pick your mode on the title screen. In Slide, the bar glides across the top of the tower and lands right where you tap. In Drop, it glides one bar higher and falls when you tap, landing wherever the swaying top has moved to by then, so aim for where the tower will be. The rules, slices, lives and speed are the same, and each mode keeps its own best.

How do I turn off the music, the sound effects or the haptics?

Open Settings with the Skins button on the title screen, or with the Settings button at the top left during a run. Under Sound, switch Music, Effects and Haptics on or off (Haptics appears on devices with a Taptic Engine). Under Motion, Reduce motion stops the screen shake and flashes; Auto follows your device's own Reduce Motion setting. The Sound button on the title screen turns the music and the effects off or on together.

How do I pick a world?

Tap Skins on the title screen to see all 13. Auto, the default, brings out each holiday world around its holiday and shows Sky Islands (listed as Classic) the rest of the year. Pick any world to stay there.

Where are my bests and medals saved?

Right on your device, in the app's own storage. There's no account and nothing is sent anywhere, so they don't move between devices, but your device's own backup (iCloud or a computer) includes them. Deleting the app deletes them.

Does it need the internet?

Not at all. Every world and every song ships inside the app, so it plays happily in Airplane Mode.

Privacy Policy

Sway Tower, the app for iPhone and iPad, and this website. Effective October 28, 2026.

In short: Sway Tower collects no data. The developer, Turk White, does not collect, receive, store, sell or share any personal information from the app.

What the app collects

Nothing. The app has no account or sign-in of its own, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no crash reporting and no server. It makes no network connections of its own and works offline. It contains no third-party code that collects data (it is built with Capacitor, an open-source app framework that runs inside the app and sends nothing anywhere), and it shares nothing with anyone.

The only ways out of the app are links you tap yourself: Support and Privacy in Settings open this website in Safari, and Rate Sway Tower shows Apple's rating prompt or opens the App Store.

What stays on your device

To work, the app saves a few things in its own storage on your device: your best score in each mode, the medals you've earned (with when and in which mode), the mode and world you picked, your settings (music, sound effects, haptics, motion), and a few reminders of its own, such as whether it has already asked you for a rating. None of it leaves your device or reaches the developer. It is included in your device's backups as your iOS settings decide, and deleting the app deletes it.

Sharing a picture of your tower

The Share button on the game-over card draws a picture of your tower and score on your device and hands it to the iOS share sheet, which sends it only where you choose. If you choose Save Image, iOS asks once for permission to add to your photo library. That permission is add-only: the app can save the picture but cannot see, read or change your photos. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Photos › Sway Tower.

Ratings

Now and then the app may show Apple's standard prompt to rate it. Apple runs that prompt; the developer sees only the public ratings and reviews on the App Store.
